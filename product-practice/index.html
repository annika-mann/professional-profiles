02 · How I decide

Good product decisions make the cut line visible.

Prioritisation is not only putting items in order. It is deciding what needs to happen now, what can wait, what we need to learn first and what we should stop doing.

MVP & scope

Outcome → Essential Journey → Risks → Evidence → Cut line.

Must haveNeeded for the outcome, usability, operations, security or compliance.
Learning itemNeeded to test a critical uncertainty before scaling further.
LaterAdds value, but the essential journey still works without it.
Not nowDoes not justify the current cost, dependency or distraction.
When something is excluded, I explain the outcome, the trade-off, the consequence and the point at which we will revisit the decision.
Cross-team dependencies

Influence starts before the dependency becomes a blocker.

I do not treat another Product Team as a delivery queue. First I clarify whether the dependency is truly hard, what outcome we need and which options exist for both teams.

01 · Align

Share the outcome early

Clarify what I need, why, by when, and what happens if it does not happen. Check whether the other team sees the same dependency.

02 · Negotiate

Open the option space

Smaller shared scope, different sequencing, workaround, delayed dependency or an adjusted MVP can all be valid outcomes.

03 · Decide

Create clarity, not victory

If priorities conflict, make impact and trade-offs visible. Evidence-based escalation is a decision mechanism, not a threat.

03 · Make the system stronger

I want the environment to learn with me.

Good product work should leave more than a feature behind: clearer context, reusable knowledge, stronger feedback loops and more distributed ownership.

01

Communicate for the situation

I choose the format based on what people need to understand, decide or move forward.

Meeting: focused alignment, conflict, ambiguity or real-time decisions Workshop: collaborative discovery, problem solving, prioritisation or shaping Chat: quick alignment and lightweight day-to-day context Email: broader context or decisions that need a clear record Ticket / doc: execution context that should remain accessible
Use synchrony where interaction adds value.
02

Make written collaboration reusable

Tickets, Confluence and customer documentation are living parts of the product system, not static handover artefacts.

Tickets with context, intent and acceptance criteria Confluence and decision docs that evolve with the product Customer documentation maintained as part of the experience Shared knowledge that reduces repeated explanation
Good writing preserves context and creates autonomy.
03

Make learning structural

I try to turn useful practices into repeatable ways of working so improvement does not depend on one person.

Stakeholder testing and validation loops Clearer QA, bug reporting and release practices Shared decision context and visible trade-offs Enablement so expertise and ownership can spread
The goal is a stronger system, not an indispensable person.
From interaction to leverage
